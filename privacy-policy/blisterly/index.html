This privacy policy applies to the Android app “Blisterly: Pill Tracker & Reminder” (“Blisterly”).

2. What Blisterly Does

Blisterly is a simple medication tracking and reminder app. It allows users to:

  • create and manage one or more medications;
  • record whether a medication intake has been confirmed for a particular day;
  • record the time of a confirmed intake;
  • keep track of the number of pills remaining in a package or container;
  • configure package sizes;
  • set local daily reminders;
  • view a local intake history; and
  • choose the app language.

Blisterly does not provide medical advice, diagnose medical conditions, calculate dosages, recommend taking an additional dose, or determine whether a medication has actually been taken.

A status such as “Not confirmed” only means that no intake confirmation has been stored in Blisterly for that day. It does not mean that the medication was definitely not taken.

3. Data Stored Locally on the Device

Blisterly stores the information required for its functions locally in the app’s private storage area on the user’s Android device.

Depending on how the user uses the app, this may include:

  • medication names entered by the user;
  • a stable internal identifier for each medication;
  • current pill count;
  • default package size;
  • daily reminder status and reminder time;
  • the date from which tracking for a medication started;
  • dates on which an intake was confirmed;
  • the time at which an intake was confirmed;
  • technical state required to keep pill counts and intake history consistent;
  • the currently selected medication where required for navigation or history display; and
  • the user’s app-language selection where an app-specific language is chosen.

These data are processed locally to provide the functions requested by the user.

RealityForge does not receive, upload, synchronize, or remotely access the medication names, pill counts, reminder settings, or intake history stored by Blisterly.

4. Health-Related Information

Medication names and intake records may reveal information concerning a person’s health and may therefore constitute health-related information.

Blisterly is deliberately designed so that this information remains on the user’s device.

Blisterly does not:

  • transmit medication data to RealityForge;
  • create a RealityForge user account;
  • store medication data in a RealityForge cloud database;
  • synchronize medication data between devices;
  • use medication data for advertising;
  • use medication data for analytics;
  • sell medication data;
  • share medication data with advertisers, data brokers, analytics providers, or other third parties; or
  • create advertising or interest profiles from medication data.

RealityForge cannot view a user’s locally stored medication information or intake history.

5. No Account, Backend, Analytics, Advertising, or Tracking

The current version of Blisterly does not use:

  • user accounts or login systems;
  • a RealityForge backend or cloud service;
  • advertising services such as Google AdMob;
  • analytics services such as Google Analytics or Firebase Analytics;
  • Firebase Crashlytics or comparable remote crash-reporting services;
  • advertising identifiers;
  • tracking pixels;
  • profiling services; or
  • third-party tracking SDKs.

The app does not request the Android INTERNET permission.

Accordingly, Blisterly itself does not transmit app data over the internet in its current version.

6. Local Notifications and Android Permissions

Blisterly can provide optional local reminders.

For this purpose, the app may request or use the following Android permissions:

POST_NOTIFICATIONS

On Android versions that require runtime permission for notifications, Blisterly asks the user for permission before notifications can be displayed.

The permission is used only to display local medication reminders and related notification actions.

Users can deny or revoke notification permission through Android settings.

RECEIVE_BOOT_COMPLETED

Blisterly uses the Android boot-completed event to restore locally configured reminder schedules after the device has restarted.

Receiving this system event does not transmit personal data to RealityForge.

Reminder scheduling and notification handling take place locally on the device.

Blisterly does not request precise location, contacts, microphone, camera, SMS, telephone, calendar, or general file-storage permissions for its core functions.

7. Notification Content

A reminder notification may display the medication name entered by the user, or the Blisterly app name if no medication name is used.

Blisterly configures reminder notifications as private so that sensitive notification content can be concealed by Android on a locked device where supported by the user’s system settings.

Users remain responsible for configuring their device and lock-screen notification settings according to their privacy preferences.

8. Local Storage and Access to the Device

Blisterly needs to store and read information on the user’s device in order to provide the functions explicitly requested by the user, including medication tracking, pill-count management, intake history, reminder configuration, and app settings.

To the extent Section 25 of the German Telecommunications-Digital-Services Data Protection Act (Telekommunikation-Digitale-Dienste-Datenschutz-Gesetz, TDDDG) applies to the storage of or access to information on the user’s device, this storage and access are strictly necessary to provide the digital service expressly requested by the user within the meaning of Section 25(2)(2) TDDDG.

Blisterly does not use local storage for advertising, cross-app tracking, or unrelated profiling.

9. Backup and Device Transfer

Blisterly is configured to disable Android cloud backup for its private app data.

The app also excludes its private app data from Android device-to-device transfer mechanisms covered by the app’s data-extraction configuration.

This is intended to keep medication and intake information on the device on which the user entered it.

Users should nevertheless protect access to their Android device using appropriate device-security settings.

10. Data Retention and Deletion

Medication and intake data remain locally stored until they are changed or deleted through normal app functions, the app’s storage is cleared through Android, or the app is uninstalled.

When a medication is deleted in Blisterly, the intake history associated with that medication is also deleted from Blisterly’s local data.

If all medications are deleted, no medication or intake-history records for those medications remain in Blisterly’s local tracker data.

Clearing Blisterly’s app data through Android settings removes the locally stored Blisterly data.

Uninstalling Blisterly removes its private app data in accordance with Android’s normal application-storage behavior.

Because RealityForge does not receive or keep a server-side copy of the medication and intake data, RealityForge cannot remotely retrieve or delete those local records on the user’s behalf.

11. Recipients and Data Sharing

Blisterly does not send its locally stored medication, intake, inventory, or reminder data to RealityForge or third parties.

There are therefore no recipients of these app data in the current version of Blisterly.

If future versions introduce online services, analytics, advertising, cloud synchronization, accounts, remote crash reporting, or other forms of data transmission, this privacy policy and any required in-app disclosures will be updated before those functions are used.

14. Data Security

Blisterly stores its tracker data in the app’s private Android storage area, which is protected by Android’s application sandbox.

The app uses atomic local file writes intended to reduce the risk of corrupted or partially written tracker data.

Android cloud backup and configured device-transfer backup of Blisterly’s private app data are disabled.

Blisterly does not transmit the locally stored medication and intake data to RealityForge.

No electronic storage system can guarantee absolute security. Users should keep their device, operating system, screen lock, and device credentials appropriately secured.

17. Children

Blisterly is not specifically directed at children.

The app does not require an account and does not transmit medication or intake data to RealityForge.

Parents or legal guardians should determine whether the app is appropriate for a minor’s personal medication-tracking needs and should supervise medication decisions where appropriate.

Blisterly does not provide medical or dosing advice to children or adults.

18. Health and Medical Disclaimer

Blisterly is a personal tracking and reminder tool.

Blisterly is not a medical device and does not diagnose, treat, cure, or prevent any medical condition.

The app does not determine whether a user should take a medication, repeat a dose, skip a dose, change a dose, or discontinue treatment.

A missing intake confirmation in Blisterly does not prove that a medication was not taken.

Users should follow the instructions provided by their physician, pharmacist, medication packaging, or other qualified healthcare professional.

For medical advice, diagnosis, treatment decisions, or uncertainty about whether a medication has already been taken, users should consult a physician, pharmacist, or other qualified healthcare professional.
